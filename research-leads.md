Historical Web Domain Discovery Strategy: Unexploited Leads, Bulk Datasets, and Autonomous Pipeline Architecture for 1996–2001 ExpansionExecutive Overview and Metric ArchitectureThe Internet Digital Ark Project mandates the autonomous, evidence-driven discovery, validation, and preservation of historical domain names active during the early expansion of the World Wide Web between calendar years 1996 and 2001. The primary goal of the current research phase is to expand the existing corpus of approximately 22 million domain-year records by achieving a cumulative growth increment of at least 5%. Acquisition performance is evaluated using the Equivalent-English Domain metric, a top-level domain (TLD) weighted sum designed to align discovery efforts with English-language web content acquisition.The Equivalent-English Domain total across the annual result files (1996.txt through 2001.txt) is calculated according to the formula:$$\text{Equivalent-English Domain Total} = \sum_{t} \left( N_t \times w_t \right)$$where $N_t$ represents the number of unique, valid domain-year records under TLD $t$ after within-year deduplication, and $w_t$ represents the fixed English primary-language share for TLD $t$ derived from the Common Crawl CC-MAIN-2024-10 model. Representative TLD weights under this model include .uk ($0.9813$), .ca ($0.8365$), .org ($0.7101$), .net ($0.6711$), .com ($0.6321$), .edu ($0.9717$), .de ($0.1324$), .nl ($0.1629$), and .fr ($0.1021$).Equivalent-English Domain Total = Sum over all TLDs (N_t * w_t)
N_t = Unique valid domain-year records under TLD t
w_t = Fixed English primary-language share for TLD t
A core constraint of the project is the strict evidentiary standard required for master file inclusion. Every domain assigned to a specific year file must be backed by item-level, verifiable temporal evidence establishing its liveness or registration during that calendar year. Evidence types designated as master-eligible include dated archive capture timestamps (cdx_timestamp), documented presence in contemporary DNS surveys (artifact_listing), domain creation or registration dates issued by authoritative registries (whois_creation), and editorial listings on contemporary dated directory artifacts (dated_directory). Unverified domains or structural links lacking explicit year-specific proof must be isolated in candidate pools (link_target).Standard interactive query routes, such as querying the public Internet Archive (IA) Wayback CDX API or public WHOIS/RDAP proxy gateways, introduce severe operational bottlenecks due to IP-level concurrency caps and rate limits (e.g., HTTP 429 and 504 errors). Direct IA CDX querying scales at approximately 500 to 510 queries per hour per IP address. To achieve the required 5% growth increment rapidly, the system must pivot away from rate-limited web interfaces toward high-throughput bulk dataset ingest, unindexed historical archive mirrors, and direct-to-registry protocol sweeping.Baseline Inventory Analysis and Evidentiary PerformanceAn analysis of the existing baseline and previously ingested sources reveals clear performance patterns regarding net-new yield, cost per unit of Equivalent-English gain, and source saturation. High-yield bulk sources—such as the Dartmouth/NBER capture census, bulk registry creation datasets, Usenet message extraction, the UK Web Archive (UKWA) Geoindex, and Network Wizards/ISC DNS survey host shards—have historically generated the majority of master-eligible additions.
