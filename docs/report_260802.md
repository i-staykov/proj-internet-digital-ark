## 4. English verification: the engine, the two sets, the audit

### The rule, and how a verdict is produced

> "a domain must belong to an English-language website or to a website where English accounts for
> more than 50% of reliably classified body text across the sampled captures for the relevant year"

Applied at website level from archived page body text, not from the domain's spelling or TLD.

Per (domain, year): ask the CDX index for in-year captures under the domain, discard candidates that
are not the site's own content, collapse URLs naming the same document, fetch the largest few
remaining as raw bytes in the Wayback `id_` form, decode with `charset_normalizer`, strip markup, and
classify with `py3langid`. Four rules inside that change the answer:

- **Raw bytes, not assumed UTF-8.** Pages of this era with undeclared charsets decode to mojibake
  under UTF-8, mojibake classifies as undetermined and leaves the denominator, which silently
  *raises* the measured English share.
- **Under 200 characters or under 0.50 confidence is not reliably classified**, and leaves the
  denominator rather than counting as non-English.
- **Captures are weighted by classified text length**, so a substantial English page outweighs a
  one-line non-English redirect notice.
- **Strictly greater than 0.50 admits**; an exact half fails, per the wording.

A pair is left **unsettled**, with no verdict written, whenever the question did not land: a failed
index query, captures that exist but could not be read, a sample truncated by a lost fetch. And "no
capture in this year" is recorded only after a second, completely unfiltered index probe, because the
normal capture query filters on `statuscode:200` and `mimetype:text/html` and a year holding only
redirects answers it empty. A domain is never excluded on the strength of a question that was not
asked.

Verdicts live in their own `domain_language` table, apart from the evidence taxonomy: evidence
answers "did this domain exist in this year", a verdict answers "what was this website". Each verdict
records **the snapshot URLs it read**, in `additions_english/<year>.csv`, so it can be refetched and
recomputed. That is what distinguishes it from a TLD prior.

### The two sets, and every exclusion

`additions_english/` and `additions_unverified/` are disjoint and together they are `additions/`. Two
integrity checks assert that, and `verify.sh` re-checks it inside the archive using only `shasum` and
`python3`. Outside the English set the `status` column separates two claims: **`disqualified`** means
the archive was asked and answered and the pair failed, one reasoned row each in
`additions_unverified/disqualified.csv`; **`unchecked`** means the engine has not reached it, and no
claim is made about its language or about whether a capture exists.

The register, so any exclusion can be inspected and disputed:

| Reason | Pairs |

`other_language` and `mixed_below_threshold` are separated so a reviewer weighing the 50% line can
see how many pairs sit near it. One row, so the format is concrete:

```
domain,year,verdict,reason,english_share,top_other,samples,snapshot_urls
100km.ch,1998,other,other_language,0.0,fr,2,https://web.archive.org/web/19981202105452id_/http://www.100km.ch:80/french/hinweise.htm https://...
```

Every accepted pair has a row in `additions/evidence_manifest.csv` carrying source URL, extraction
method, evidence type and the dated evidence value; the invariant `evidence_wall_intact` makes an
unevidenced assignment inexpressible.

### Throughput, and what the number will be

**Measured: 356 pairs per hour** over 11 complete batches (4,400 pairs in
741 minutes), with **58.8% of settled verdicts coming back English**. More concurrency
buys nothing: 3 workers measured slower than 2, the third such measurement in this project, because
the limit is what the archive serves a single client.

The engine runs until Monday 3 August 12:00 UTC, 34 hours from this snapshot. At 356
pairs/hour that is roughly 12,000 more pairs read, about 7,000 additions at the measured
share, or 5,000 with a 70% duty allowance for the archive refusing this project, which it has
done three times. The lower figure is the planning number, and this is arithmetic rather than
commitment.

**The ceiling:** 24,148 of the 151,949 additions have a known in-year capture. That subset
alone is about three days of continuous querying and the full backlog roughly two weeks, so this is a
multi-week job at the pace the archive permits. Neither figure claims the rest have no capture, only
that they have not been asked about.

### Audited, and versioned

Two adversarial review passes were run over the engine on 1 August, briefed in opposite directions:
one to find pairs that could enter the English files wrongly, one to find pairs that could be wrongly
excluded. **Ten defects were confirmed**, each reproduced before being accepted; the full findings
ship as `audit/engine_review.md`. The most serious was a provenance failure: `web.archive.org`
answers a replay it cannot serve exactly with the nearest capture **in any year**, and the engine
recorded the URL it had *asked* for, so a reviewer refetching it would have received the same
substitution and seen agreement. An audit trail that confirms its own error is worse than none. The
fetcher now stores the URL that answered, and out-of-year samples are discarded.

**Every verdict held at that point was discarded rather than shipped**, and coverage rebuilt from
zero on the corrected engine; the superseded journals ship under `journals/lang_superseded/`. Three
rules now stop a defect becoming permanent: every verdict carries the `ENGINE_VERSION` that produced
it and only current-version verdicts reach an annual file; a pair leaves the work queue only when
asking again could not change the answer (`no_capture_in_year` is the one final rejection, because
the archive's index for a past year does not grow); and a verdict reached on a truncated sample does
not settle the pair.

### Sources assessed this round

Two adopted, three rejected after measurement. All five are in `sources.md` with their measurements,
including the negative results.

| Source | Outcome | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Usenet announcement archives | adopted | 115,986 net-new pairs, see below |
| Tucows software catalogue | adopted | 942 net-new pairs on 878 domains |
| NYPW first-capture index | rejected | 53 net-new domains measured against 27,276 estimated |
| Australian Web Archive (PANDORA) | rejected | 0 in-window captures in a clean 60-domain sample |
| ISP customer-directory vein | rejected | 5 net-new domains measured against 1,000 to 5,000 estimated |

**Adopted: Usenet announcement archives.** Giganews donated its Usenet archive to the Internet
Archive, and announcement groups carry a posting date beside the URLs in each message. The date is
intrinsic to the artifact rather than recovered from a crawl, which is what lets the source reach
years the archive's own coverage does not.

The admission rule exists because a URL in a message body is typed by a human. A domain another
source already places in an annual file is real and only its year is open, so the post dates it; a
name appearing only in Usenet becomes a candidate and must earn its own evidence. Measured: of the
Usenet-sourced pairs in the annual files, **zero** rest on a domain no other source attests. The rule
is worth its cost: in a 4,000-name sample, 35.4% of never-before-seen Usenet names are within one
edit of a name already held, an upper bound on typo contamination large enough to justify the split.

**Rejected: NYPW first-capture index.** The 27,276-domain estimate compared registered domains
against raw hostname lines; measured against the store, all but **53** of its 2.35M in-window domains
are already held, a 99.998% overlap. Two minutes of measurement avoided a 19.35 GB download.

**Rejected: Australian Web Archive.** A clean 60-domain sample returned zero transport failures and
zero in-window captures; PANDORA's selective harvesting is simply later than this window for the long
tail.

Three of five estimates were contradicted by measurement, two by two orders of magnitude, which is
why every figure in this report is measured against the store or a recorded run, and why the
measuring scripts ship.

## 7. The infrastructure this builds on, briefly

**The evidence wall.** `domain_year.evidence_id` is `NOT NULL` and references a specific evidence
row, so an unevidenced year assignment cannot be expressed. Anything with evidence of existence but
not of a year goes to the candidate pool.

**Counting unit.** The registered domain under SPEC III.8, via a pinned Public Suffix List snapshot.

**Reproducibility, in three tiers.** Verify the shipped result from checksums and the evidence
manifest (about 10 s, no network); rebuild every result file byte-identically from the shipped
Parquet evidence graph (about 1 min, no source data); or rebuild from the original sources (a
download, then about 20 minutes). The archive's `README.md` gives the commands, and all three were
run against this archive from an unrelated directory before it shipped.

**The integrity gate.** Twelve invariants over the store, run by `ark check`, non-zero exit on any
failure. All twelve pass on the shipped data.

## 8. Limitations

- **English verification covers a small share of the additions.** Section 4 gives the rate and the
  ceiling; the set grows continuously, and the figure in section 1 is what shipped.
- **The genuinely new domains are the smaller, stricter claim.** 1,252 domains are absent from
  the baseline in every year; the headline pair count is mostly years gained on domains it already
  holds, which is what gap-filling produces.
- **Usenet URLs are human-typed.** The corroboration split is the mitigation, and it is why tens of
  thousands of Usenet-discovered names sit in the candidate pool rather than the annual files.
- **A verdict is a point-in-time reading of specific pages** in the year's captures, not proof the
  site was live or unchanged all year. The snapshot URLs ship so any verdict can be re-examined.
- **Image-heavy pages are under-served**: `alt` text is deliberately not collected, being frequently
  English boilerplate on non-English sites. A false exclusion stays retryable; a false admission does
  not.
- **Captures below the 200-character threshold are not pooled**, so a site with three 150-character
  English pages scores undetermined. Found in the audit, deferred because it changes what a verdict
  would be; first in the queue for the next round.
