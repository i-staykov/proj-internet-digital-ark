## 4. The English-website verification engine

### The rule

> "a domain must belong to an English-language website or to a website where English accounts for
> more than 50% of reliably classified body text across the sampled captures for the relevant year"

Applied at website level from archived page body text, not from the domain's spelling or TLD.

### How a verdict is produced

Per (domain, year): ask the CDX index for in-year captures under the domain, discard candidates that
are not the site's own content, collapse URLs naming the same document, take the largest few
remaining, fetch them as **raw bytes** in the Wayback `id_` form so the archive's own page furniture is
never in the sample, decode with `charset_normalizer`, strip markup to body text, classify with
`py3langid`.

Four rules inside that change the answer:

- **Raw bytes, not assumed UTF-8.** 1990s pages are often latin-1, Shift-JIS or GB2312 with no declared
  charset. As UTF-8 they become mojibake, mojibake classifies as undetermined, and undetermined pages
  leave the denominator, which silently *raises* the measured English share.
- **Under 200 characters or under 0.50 confidence is not reliably classified**, and leaves the
  denominator rather than counting as non-English.
- **Captures are weighted by classified text length**, so a substantial English page outweighs a
  one-line non-English redirect notice.
- **Strictly greater than 0.50 admits.** An exact half fails, per the wording.

### What the engine refuses to decide

A pair is left **unsettled**, with no verdict written, whenever the question did not land: the index
query failed, captures existed and none could be read, or the sample was truncated by a lost fetch.
Recording those as "undetermined" would exclude a possibly-English domain on a transport error.

**"No capture in this year" is recorded only after asking without filters.** The capture query filters
on `statuscode:200` and `mimetype:text/html`, so a year holding only redirects answers it empty. An
empty filtered result triggers a second, completely unfiltered probe, with three outcomes: nothing at
all (`no_capture_in_year`), something unreadable (`no_readable_html_capture`), or a failed probe, which
leaves the pair unsettled. Both counts are in the register in section 7.

### Language is not an evidence type

Verdicts go to a separate `domain_language` table. Every `evidence_type` answers "did this domain exist
in this year"; a verdict answers "what was this website", and a domain can be perfectly evidenced and
still inadmissible. Each verdict records **the snapshot URLs it read**, in
`additions_english/<year>.csv`, so it can be refetched and recomputed. That is what distinguishes it
from a TLD prior.

## 5. Throughput, and what the number will be

**Measured: 356 pairs per hour**, over 11 complete batches of the corrected engine
(4,400 pairs in 741 minutes). **58.8% of settled verdicts come back English**,
between roughly a half and two thirds by batch, because the queue interleaves the early years, which
yield less.

**More concurrency buys nothing, measured rather than assumed.** Two batches at 2 workers with a 1.5 s
floor ran at 367 and 381 pairs/hour; one batch at 3 workers with a 1.2 s floor ran at **364**, at the
same throttle count and equally close to the governor's floor. The limit is what the archive serves a
single client. This is the third time in this project that pushing concurrency at `web.archive.org`
has bought nothing.

**Projection, arithmetic rather than commitment.** The engine runs until Monday 3 August 12:00 UTC,
35 hours from this snapshot. At 356 pairs/hour that is roughly 12,500 more pairs read,
which at the measured share is about 7,500 additions, or 5,000 with a 70% duty allowance
for the archive refusing this project. It has done so three times, twice overnight, and the
supervisor then backs off for up to 30 minutes, so the lower figure is the planning number.

**The ceiling.** 24,148 of the 151,949 additions have a known in-year capture. At 356
pairs/hour that subset alone is about three days of continuous querying and the full backlog roughly
two weeks. This is a multi-week job at the pace the archive permits. Neither figure claims the rest
have no capture, only that they have not been asked about.

### Sources assessed this round

Two adopted, three rejected after measurement. All five are in `sources.md` with their measurements,
including the negative results.

| Source | Outcome | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Usenet announcement archives | adopted | 115,986 net-new pairs, see below |
| Tucows software catalogue | adopted | 942 net-new pairs on 878 domains |
| NYPW first-capture index | rejected | 53 net-new domains measured against 27,276 estimated |
| Australian Web Archive (PANDORA) | rejected | 0 in-window captures in a clean 60-domain sample |
| ISP customer-directory vein | rejected | 5 net-new domains measured against 1,000 to 5,000 estimated |

**Adopted: Usenet announcement archives.** Giganews donated its Usenet archive to the Internet
Archive. Announcement and commerce groups carry a posting date beside the URLs in each message, and
that date is intrinsic to the artifact rather than recovered from a crawl, which is what lets the
source reach years the archive's own coverage does not.

The admission rule exists because a URL in a message body is typed by a human. A domain another source
already places in an annual file is real and only its year is open, so the post dates it
(`dated_directory`); a name appearing only in Usenet has neither its existence nor its year attested,
so it becomes a candidate and must earn its own evidence. The test is "appears in `domain_year`", not
"appears in `domain`", or a typo recorded as a candidate by an earlier round would corroborate itself.
Measured: of the Usenet-sourced pairs in the annual files, **zero** rest on a domain no other source
attests.

The risk that motivated the rule: in a 4,000-name sample, 35.4% of never-before-seen Usenet names are
within one edit of a name the store already holds. That is an **upper bound on typo contamination, not
an estimate of it**, since with 5.5M domains held a large share of any name list is within one edit of
something. It is quoted because it was large enough to justify the split.

Two findings worth carrying forward. The donation rewrote a large share of `Date:` headers as a bare
`YYYY/MM/DD`, which `email.utils.parsedate_to_datetime` rejects outright, in **21,346 of 23,282
messages** in `comp.infosystems.www.announce`; before that was handled the route looked worth 913
pairs with nothing before 2000, after it 6,885 across all six years from the same files. And group
size does not predict in-window content: `alt.www.webmaster` is 170 MB and entirely 2006 to 2013, so
groups are now selected by name and expected yield.

**Rejected: NYPW first-capture index, estimated 27,276 net-new domains, measured 53.** The estimate
compared NYPW's registered domains against raw hostname lines from the first baseline, and both errors
inflate. Measured against the store: 2,413,003 in-window pairs over 2,354,914 domains, all but **53**
already held, a 99.998% overlap, which is what a sample of the Internet Archive's own CDX should look
like against a baseline drawn from it. Two minutes of measurement avoided a 19.35 GB download.
Reproducible via `scripts/measure_nypw_yield.py`.

**Rejected: Australian Web Archive, 0 of 60 sampled domains with an in-window capture.** The endpoint
correction is worth keeping even though the source failed: `webarchive.nla.gov.au/awa/cdx` serves an
anti-bot challenge but **`web.archive.org.au/awa/cdx` answers normally**, so the earlier rejection in
`sources.md` was stale. The pool looked strong, 29,595 PANDORA domains in no annual file, and a clean
60-domain sample returned zero transport failures and zero in-window captures. PANDORA's selective
harvesting is simply later than this window for the long tail.

**Method note.** Three of five sources assessed were rejected after measurement contradicted the
estimate, two by two orders of magnitude. Every figure in this report is therefore measured against
the store or a recorded run, and the measuring scripts ship. Where a figure is a projection, section 5
labels it in the same sentence.

## 7. The two shipped sets, and every exclusion

`additions_english/` and `additions_unverified/` are disjoint and together they are `additions/`. Two
integrity checks assert that, and `verify.sh` re-checks it inside the archive using only `shasum` and
`python3`.

A pair sits outside the English set for one of two very different reasons:

- **`disqualified`**: the archive was asked and answered, and the pair failed. Every one carries a
  reason and a row in `additions_unverified/disqualified.csv`.
- **`unchecked`**: the engine has not reached it. **No claim is made about its language, and none
  about whether the archive holds a capture for it.**

### The disqualification register

Every judged rejection is listed individually with the pages read, so any exclusion can be inspected
and disputed.

| Reason | Pairs |
|---|--:|
| `insufficient_text` | 736 |
| `other_language` | 392 |
| `no_capture_in_year` | 380 |
| `no_readable_html_capture` | 78 |
| `mixed_below_threshold` | 53 |
| `non_site_text` | 47 |

`other_language` and `mixed_below_threshold` are separated so a reviewer weighing whether the 50% line
sits in the right place can see how many pairs are near it rather than nowhere near it. One row, so
the format is concrete:

```
domain,year,verdict,reason,english_share,top_other,samples,snapshot_urls
100km.ch,1998,other,other_language,0.0,fr,2,https://web.archive.org/web/19981202105452id_/http://www.100km.ch:80/french/hinweise.htm https://...
```

A Swiss site, two archived pages read, no English in either, and both URLs given so the judgement can
be checked in a browser.

### Fields preserved for every accepted pair

One row per pair in `additions/evidence_manifest.csv`, carrying source URL, extraction method,
evidence type and evidence value. **What plays the role of the timestamp differs by evidence type, and
the manifest names which:** a 14-digit Wayback capture timestamp for `cdx_timestamp`, a Message-ID
identifying a dated post for Usenet `dated_directory` rows, a registry creation date for
`whois_creation`, a dated artifact identifier for the survey and directory sources. For Usenet the URL
is the archive collection rather than the individual post, because the donation is published per group
and not per message.

The invariant `evidence_wall_intact` makes absence impossible: `domain_year.evidence_id` is `NOT NULL`
and references a specific evidence row, so an unevidenced assignment cannot be expressed.

## 9. The engine is audited, and verdicts are versioned

Two adversarial passes were run over the verification engine on 1 August, briefed in opposite
directions: one to find pairs that could reach the English files wrongly, one to find pairs that could
be wrongly excluded. **Ten defects were confirmed**, each reproduced before being accepted and the
most serious checked against live archived pages. Full findings ship as `audit/engine_review.md`.

The one worth stating here is a provenance failure rather than a classifier failure.
`web.archive.org` answers a replay it cannot serve exactly with a redirect to the nearest capture in
time, **in any year**, and the client followed it silently. Because the engine recorded the URL it had
*asked* for, a reviewer refetching it would have received the same substitution and seen agreement.
**An audit trail that confirms its own error is worse than none.** The fetcher now reports which URL
answered, out-of-year samples are discarded, and the stored URL is the one that was served.

**Every verdict held at that point was discarded rather than shipped**, and coverage rebuilt from zero
on the corrected engine. The superseded journals ship under `journals/lang_superseded/` with a note on
what was wrong with each version, so a discarded verdict stays reproducible rather than merely
deleted.

Three rules now stop a defect becoming permanent:

- Every verdict carries the `ENGINE_VERSION` that produced it, and only current-version verdicts can
  reach an annual file.
- A pair leaves the work queue only when asking again could not change the answer. The exception is
  `no_capture_in_year`, final because the archive's index for a past year does not grow.
- A verdict reached on a truncated sample does not settle the pair.

## 10. The infrastructure this builds on, briefly

**The evidence wall.** `domain_year.evidence_id` is `NOT NULL` and references a specific evidence row,
so an unevidenced year assignment cannot be expressed. Evidence types are split into master-eligible
and candidate-only, and the assignment helper refuses candidate-only evidence, so anything with
evidence of existence but not of a year goes to the candidate pool.

**Counting unit.** The registered domain under SPEC III.8, via a pinned Public Suffix List snapshot, so
the result does not depend on the day it was run.

**Reproducibility, in three tiers.** Verify the shipped result from checksums and the evidence manifest
(about 10 s, no network); rebuild the annual files and both shipped sets from the Parquet evidence
graph, byte-identically (about 1 min, no source data); or rebuild from the original sources (a
download, then about 20 minutes). The archive's `README.md` gives the commands, and all three were run
against this archive from an unrelated directory before it shipped.

**The integrity gate.** Twelve invariants over the store, run by `ark check`, non-zero exit on any
failure. All twelve pass on the shipped data.

## 11. Limitations

- **English verification covers a small share of the additions.** Section 5 gives the rate and the
  ceiling. The set will grow; the figure in section 1 is what shipped.
- **The genuinely new domains are the smaller and stricter claim.** 1,252 domains are absent
  from the baseline in every year; the headline pair count is mostly years gained on domains it
  already holds, which is what gap-filling produces by construction.
- **Usenet URLs are human-typed.** The corroboration split is the mitigation, and it is why tens of
  thousands of Usenet-discovered names sit in the candidate pool rather than in the annual files.
- **A capture proves the site was archived that year, not that it was live all year.**
- **Verdicts are point-in-time readings of specific pages.** A site sampled at two captures may have
  differed at a third. The snapshot URLs ship so any verdict can be re-examined.
- **Image-only pages are under-served.** Text in `alt` attributes is deliberately not collected: it is
  often English boilerplate on non-English sites, so admitting it would bias toward admission. The
  cost is that a genuinely English image-heavy page may read as having no text. Judged the safer error,
  since a false exclusion stays retryable and a false admission does not.
- **Captures below the 200-character threshold are not pooled.** A site with three 150-character
  English pages scores undetermined where the same text on one page would not. Found during the audit
  and not changed mid-round, because it alters what a verdict would be; it is first in the queue for
  the next round.

## 12. What is worth doing next

1. **Keep the verification engine running.** It is the binding constraint on the admissible set and the
   only thing that converts existing work into shippable additions.
2. **Verify the candidate pool into net-new domains.** 112,946 names, tens of thousands of them
   Usenet-discovered and never seen by this project; a capture is what promotes one. This is the only
   route that moves the strict net-new-domain count, and it was stopped this round only because it
   competes with English verification for the same host. The measured hit rate on the general pool is
   62%, but it has **not** been measured on the Usenet-discovered subset, the least crawl-attested
   population in the store, so measure that before budgeting the route.
3. **More sources with dates intrinsic to the artifact.** The Usenet result generalises: what worked
   was not Usenet, it was that the date does not depend on a crawl. Anything with a dated record and a
   URL beside it deserves the same assessment, and most of the shortlisted Usenet groups are still
   unprocessed.
