# Internet Digital Ark: round report, 1 August 2026

Additions to the 1996-2001 annual domain lists, measured against **merged260730**, and the
English-website verification this round introduces.

This report covers what is new since the 26 July delivery. The pipeline it builds on is summarised in
one section rather than re-described, because that ground was covered in the previous report and
reviewed.

**Where the figures come from.** Store figures (additions, verdicts, per-source counts, completeness
denominators) are emitted by `scripts/report_figures.py`, which ships in the archive; running it
reproduces every table below. Source assessments come from `scripts/measure_nypw_yield.py`,
`scripts/measure_usenet_yield.py` and `scripts/measure_capture_rate.py`, also shipped. Throughput
figures come from `logs/lang_supervisor.log`. The engine audit is recorded in `audit/engine_review.md`.
Nothing here is quoted without one of those behind it.

**Snapshot.** All figures are taken at the moment the archive was built; the verification engine runs
continuously, so a later snapshot will show a larger English set and nothing else changed.

## 2. What this round added, per year

[PER_YEAR_TABLE]

**Capture-backed** is the column that predicts what can become admissible, and it is why the English
counts are distributed as they are. It means our own evidence already names an in-year Internet
Archive capture for that exact pair, so there is body text to read. The verification queue works
those pairs first, because a request spent on a pair with no capture usually returns nothing.

**That has an uncomfortable consequence worth stating plainly.** 1996 and 1997 are the years the
feedback most wants expanded and the years closest to the completeness threshold, and they are also
the years with almost no capture-backed pairs, because their additions are dated by Usenet posts
rather than by crawls. Under a strict capture-backed ordering they would stay at zero English
verdicts however long the engine ran.

So we measured rather than assumed. `scripts/measure_capture_rate.py` sampled 200 of those pairs and
asked the archive's index directly, without filters: **5.4% of 1996 and 12.6% of 1997 do have an
in-year capture, 9.1% overall.** An earlier sample of pre-Usenet 1996 domains had returned zero, so
the population genuinely changed when Usenet brought in domains that someone had thought worth
announcing. On that measurement the two years are worth a minority share of the budget rather than
none, and the queue now threads one early-year pair in for every ten capture-backed ones. The trade
is roughly 65 verdicts gained in those years against 320 given up elsewhere.

## 4. The English-website verification engine

### What the rule is, and what it is applied to

> "a domain must belong to an English-language website or to a website where English accounts for
> more than 50% of reliably classified body text across the sampled captures for the relevant year"

Applied at website level, from archived page body text, explicitly not from the spelling of the domain
or its TLD.

### How a verdict is produced

Per (domain, year): ask the CDX index for in-year captures anywhere under the domain, discard
candidates that are not the website's own content, collapse URLs naming the same document, take the
largest few remaining, fetch them as **raw bytes** in the Wayback `id_` form so the archive's own page
furniture is never in the sample, decode with `charset_normalizer` rather than assuming UTF-8, strip
markup to body text, and classify with `py3langid`.

Four decisions inside that change the answer:

- **Raw bytes, not UTF-8 text.** 1990s pages are frequently latin-1, Shift-JIS or GB2312 with no
  declared charset. Decoded as UTF-8 they become mojibake, mojibake classifies as undetermined, and
  undetermined pages leave the denominator, which would silently *raise* the measured English share.
- **Under 200 characters, or under 0.50 confidence, is "not reliably classified"** and leaves the
  denominator entirely rather than counting as non-English. Counting them would drag genuinely English
  sites out.
- **Captures are weighted by classified text length**, so a substantial English page outweighs a
  one-line non-English redirect notice instead of each counting once.
- **Strictly greater than 0.50 admits.** An exact half fails, per the wording.

### Language is not an evidence type

Verdicts go to a separate `domain_language` table rather than into the evidence taxonomy. Every
`evidence_type` answers "did this domain exist in this year"; a language verdict answers "what was
this website", which is orthogonal, and a domain can be perfectly evidenced and still inadmissible.

Each verdict records **the exact snapshot URLs it read**, in `additions_english/<year>.csv`. Any
verdict in this submission can be refetched and recomputed by the reader, which is what distinguishes
it from a TLD prior.

### What the engine refuses to decide

A pair is left **unsettled**, with no verdict written at all, whenever the question did not land: the
index query failed, or captures existed and none could be read, or the sample was truncated by a lost
fetch. Recording any of those as "undetermined" would permanently exclude a possibly-English domain on
the strength of a transport error. This project made that exact mistake once before, in the RDAP
engine, at a cost of 12,888 domains.

**And "the archive holds no capture for this domain in this year" is only recorded after asking that
question without filters.** The capture query filters on `statuscode:200` and `mimetype:text/html`, so
a year holding only redirects answers it empty. Treating that as "nothing exists" would disqualify a
domain on a question that was never asked, so an empty filtered result triggers a second, completely
unfiltered index probe. Three outcomes, all measured: nothing at all (`no_capture_in_year`), something
but not readable HTML (`no_readable_html_capture`), or the probe itself failed, in which case the pair
stays unsettled.

## 5. Coverage, throughput, and what the number will be

The English count is bound by how fast `web.archive.org` will answer, not by anything in the pipeline.

**Measured throughput.** 1,200 pairs in 209 minutes, **344 pairs/hour**, while a second engine
competed for the same host. Stopping that engine and giving the whole budget to verification moved it
to **429 pairs/hour** at unchanged settings, which is the clearest evidence that contention rather
than concurrency was the binding variable. The corrected engine then measured **375 pairs/hour**:
deliberately slower, because it sends an extra index probe on the roughly quarter of pairs whose
filtered query returns nothing, and walks past unreadable captures instead of settling on them.

**Planning arithmetic, and it is arithmetic rather than a commitment.** Section 6 of this report
argues that estimates in this domain are unreliable, and this one is subject to the same caution: at
375 pairs/hour the engine reads roughly 18,000 more pairs in 48 hours, and at the observed English
share that is a few thousand additions. The archive has refused this project three times, twice
overnight, and the supervisor backs off for up to 30 minutes when it does, so the low end of any range
is the sensible planning figure.

**Two ceilings worth stating together.**

- **[CAPTUREBACKED] of the [TOTAL] additions have a known in-year capture.** At 375 pairs/hour that
  subset alone is about three days of continuous querying; the full backlog is roughly two weeks.
  English verification of this collection is a multi-week job at the pace the archive permits.
- The capture-backed share fell from 66.7% last round to [CBSHARE] now, and that is the Usenet route
  working rather than a regression. It added [USENETPAIRS] pairs dated by a post rather than by a
  crawl, which grows the denominator of a ratio about crawls.

Neither figure is a claim that the remaining pairs have no capture. It means only that we have not
asked. A pair we have not asked about is reported as `unchecked`, never as a rejection.

### Adopted: Usenet announcement archives

Giganews donated its Usenet archive to the Internet Archive. Announcement and commerce groups carry a
posting date beside the URLs in each message, and the date is intrinsic to the artifact rather than
recovered from a crawl. That is what lets it reach years the archive's own coverage does not.

**Admission rule, because a URL in a message body is typed by a human.** A domain another source
already places in an annual file is real and only its year is open, so the post dates it
(`dated_directory`). A name appearing only in Usenet has neither its existence nor its year
independently attested, so it becomes a candidate and must earn its own evidence. The test is
"appears in `domain_year`", not "appears in `domain`", or a typo recorded as a candidate by an earlier
round would corroborate itself.

**The rule is measured, not just stated.** Of the Usenet-sourced pairs in the annual files, **zero**
rest on a domain that no other source attests.

**On the risk that motivated the rule.** In a 4,000-name sample, 35.4% of never-before-seen Usenet
names are within one edit of a name the store already holds. That is an **upper bound on typo
contamination, not an estimate of it**: with 5.5M domains held, a large share of any name list is
within one edit of something. It is quoted because it was large enough to justify the corroboration
split, not as a measurement of how many names are wrong. The sample was the alphabetically first
4,000, which skews toward short and numeric names, so it is not a population rate either.

**A parser finding worth carrying to anyone else using this corpus.** The donation rewrote a large
share of `Date:` headers as a bare `YYYY/MM/DD`, which `email.utils.parsedate_to_datetime` rejects
outright: **21,346 of 23,282 messages** in `comp.infosystems.www.announce`. Before that was handled
the route looked worth 913 pairs with nothing before 2000; after it, 6,885 across all six years from
the same files. A source can appear barren purely because of a header format.

Group size does not predict in-window content. `alt.www.webmaster` is 170 MB and entirely 2006 to
2013, so groups are now selected by name and expected yield rather than by size.

### Rejected: NYPW first-capture index. Estimated 27,276 net-new domains, measured 53

The estimate compared NYPW's **registered domains** against **raw hostname lines** from the
**phase-1** baseline. Both errors inflate. Measured against the current store: 2,413,003 in-window
pairs over 2,354,914 domains, of which all but **53** are already held, a 99.998% overlap, which is
what a sample of the Internet Archive's own CDX should look like against a baseline already drawn from
it. Two minutes of measurement avoided a 19.35 GB download. Reproducible via
`scripts/measure_nypw_yield.py`.

### Rejected: Australian Web Archive. 0 of 60 sampled domains had an in-window capture

The endpoint correction is worth keeping even though the source failed:
`webarchive.nla.gov.au/awa/cdx` serves an anti-bot challenge, but **`web.archive.org.au/awa/cdx`
answers normally**, so the earlier rejection in `sources.md` was stale. The pool looked strong, 29,595
PANDORA domains in no annual file, and a clean 60-domain sample returned zero transport failures and
zero in-window captures. PANDORA's selective harvesting is simply later than our window for the long
tail.

### Method note

Three of the five sources assessed were rejected after measurement contradicted the estimate, two of
them by two orders of magnitude. Estimates in this space are unreliable enough that every figure in
this report is measured against the store, and the measuring scripts ship so the numbers can be
re-derived.

## 7. The two shipped sets, and every exclusion

`additions_english/` and `additions_unverified/` are disjoint and together they are `additions/`. Two
integrity checks assert disjointness and that nothing admissible was left out of the admitted set;
`verify.sh` additionally checks that the two sets sum to `additions/`, from inside the archive, with
no dependency beyond `python3`.

A pair sits outside the English set for one of two very different reasons:

- **`disqualified`**: the archive was asked and answered, and the pair failed the standard. Every one
  carries a reason and a row in `additions_unverified/disqualified.csv`.
- **`unchecked`**: the engine has not reached it. **No claim is made about its language, and none
  about whether the archive holds a capture for it.**

### The disqualification register

Every judged rejection is listed individually with the pages that were read, so any exclusion can be
inspected and disputed. The vocabulary is closed, and the counts are given because the point of
separating them is to let a reviewer weigh the threshold:

[REASON_TABLE]

`other_language` and `mixed_below_threshold` are separated so that a reviewer weighing whether the 50%
line sits in the right place can see how many pairs are near it rather than nowhere near it.

### Fields preserved for every accepted pair

Source URL, archive timestamp, extraction method and evidence type, one row per pair in
`additions/evidence_manifest.csv`. The invariant `evidence_wall_intact` enforces that none can be
absent: `domain_year.evidence_id` is `NOT NULL` and references a specific evidence row, so an
unevidenced assignment cannot be expressed.

## 9. The engine was audited, and what that found

Two adversarial review passes were run over the verification engine on 1 August, briefed in opposite
directions: one to find pairs that could reach the English files wrongly, one to find pairs that could
be wrongly excluded. **Ten defects were confirmed**, each reproduced before being accepted and the
most serious checked against live archived pages. The full findings ship as `audit/engine_review.md`.

The one worth reading in full here is a provenance failure rather than a classifier failure.
`web.archive.org` answers a replay request it cannot serve exactly with a redirect to the nearest
capture in time, **in any year**, and the client followed it silently and reported success. Verified
live: a request for the 1997 capture of `1697.com` returned the capture of 17 October 2000. The engine
recorded the URL it had *asked* for, so a reviewer refetching it would have received the same
substitution and seen agreement. **The audit trail would have confirmed the error rather than exposed
it**, which is the one property a provenance record must never have. The fetcher now reports which URL
answered, out-of-year samples are discarded, and the stored URL is the one that was served.

The other nine, in one line each: selection preferred `robots.txt` and vendor webmail pages over site
content; placeholder detection skipped any page over 1,000 characters, admitting a keyword link farm
and a forwarding notice; a truncated sample could settle a verdict, which had happened to 124 of 839
English verdicts; a filtered query's emptiness was recorded as "no capture"; duplicate documents
consumed the sample; escaped markup was counted as prose; gzip-encoded captures decoded to noise;
`--samples 0` would have settled the whole work list; and underneath all of them, nothing could ever
re-judge a pair, which is what made each defect permanent at the moment it produced output.

**Every verdict held at that point was discarded rather than shipped**, and none of it ever left this
project. Coverage returned to zero and rebuilt on the corrected engine. Their journals ship under
`journals/lang_superseded/` with a note on what was wrong with each version, so a discarded verdict
remains reproducible rather than merely deleted. The same decision was taken once before in this
project, for the same reason: the entire claim of this route is that a verdict is checkable, and a
checkable verdict that is wrong is worse than none.

Verified live after the fixes: the link farm and the forwarding notice are now rejected as non-site
text, the `robots.txt` pair reads seven real pages instead, and `1stflatrate.com` remains English but
on the site's own pages rather than a mail login. A correct admission kept on correct evidence is what
distinguishes a fix from a blanket tightening.

Verdicts now carry the version of the engine that produced them, only current-version verdicts can
enter an annual file, and a pair leaves the work queue only when asking again could not change the
answer.

## 10. The infrastructure this builds on, briefly

**The evidence wall.** `domain_year.evidence_id` is `NOT NULL` and references a specific evidence row,
so an unevidenced year assignment cannot be expressed. Evidence types are split into master-eligible
and candidate-only, and the assignment helper refuses candidate-only evidence. Anything with evidence
of existence but not of a year goes to the candidate pool and stays out of the annual files.

**Counting unit.** The registered domain under SPEC III.8, via a pinned Public Suffix List snapshot so
the result does not depend on the day it was run.

**Reproducibility, in three tiers.** Verify the shipped result from checksums and the evidence
manifest (about 10 s, no network); rebuild the annual files and both shipped sets from the Parquet
evidence graph, byte-identically (about 1 min, no source data); or rebuild from the original sources
(a download, then about 20 minutes). The archive's `README.md` gives the commands, and all three were
run against this archive from a directory unrelated to the project before it shipped.

**The integrity gate.** Twelve invariants over the store, run by `ark check`, non-zero exit on any
failure. All twelve pass on the shipped data. Three of them concern the English sets; with a small
English set those three are satisfied over few rows, so they constrain less than they will later.

## 11. Limitations

- **English verification covers a small share of the additions.** Section 5 gives the rate and the
  ceiling. The set will grow; the figure in section 1 is what shipped.
- **The genuinely new domains are the smaller and stricter claim.** [NEWDOMAINS] domains are absent
  from the baseline in every year. The headline pair count is mostly years gained on domains the
  baseline already holds, which is what gap-filling produces by construction.
- **Usenet URLs are human-typed.** The corroboration split is the mitigation, and it is why tens of
  thousands of Usenet-discovered names sit in the candidate pool rather than in the annual files.
- **A capture proves the site was archived that year, not that it was live all year.** The evidence
  supports presence, not continuous operation.
- **Verdicts are point-in-time readings of specific pages.** A site sampled at two captures may have
  differed at a third. The snapshot URLs ship so any verdict can be re-examined.
- **Image-only pages are under-served.** Text in `alt` attributes is deliberately not collected: it is
  often English boilerplate on non-English sites, so admitting it would bias toward admission. The
  cost is that a genuinely English image-heavy page may read as having no text. Judged the safer
  error, since a false exclusion stays retryable and a false admission does not.
- **Captures below the 200-character threshold are not pooled.** A site with three 150-character
  English pages scores undetermined where the same text on one page would not. Identified during the
  audit and deliberately not changed mid-round, because it alters what a verdict would be; it is the
  first change queued for the next round.

## 12. What is worth doing next

1. **Keep the verification engine running.** It is the binding constraint on the admissible set and
   the only thing that converts existing work into shippable additions. At the measured rate the
   capture-backed subset is about three days and the full backlog about two weeks.
2. **Verify the candidate pool into net-new domains.** [CANDIDATES] names, tens of thousands of them
   Usenet-discovered and never seen by this project. A capture is what promotes one. The measured hit
   rate on the general candidate pool is 62%; it has **not** been measured on the Usenet-discovered
   subset, which is the least crawl-attested population in the store, so that number should be
   measured before the route is budgeted. This is the only route that moves the strict net-new-domain
   count, and it was stopped this round only because it competes with English verification for the
   same host.
3. **More sources with dates intrinsic to the artifact.** The Usenet result generalises: what worked
   was not Usenet, it was that the date does not depend on a crawl. Anything with a dated record and a
   URL beside it deserves the same assessment, and most of the shortlisted Usenet groups are still
   unprocessed.
