# Internet Digital Ark: round report, 1 August 2026

Additions to the 1996-2001 annual domain lists, measured against **merged260730**, and the
English-website verification this round introduces.

This report covers **what is new since the 26 July delivery**. The pipeline it builds on is
summarised in one section rather than re-described, because that ground was covered in the previous
report and reviewed. Everything quoted here is derived from `data/ark.duckdb` by
`scripts/report_figures.py`, which ships in the archive, so any figure can be recomputed rather than
believed.

## 1. Summary

Two things changed this round, one in what we collect and one in what we ship.

**We now verify the English-website standard from archived page text**, per (domain, year), and
record for every verdict the exact snapshot URLs it was read from. Feedback section 6 asks for the
provisional TLD-stratified estimate to be replaced with archived-content evidence, and this is that
replacement. It is a measurement, not an inference from the domain name or its TLD.

**The deliverable is now two disjoint sets.** English-verified additions and everything else, in
separate files, summing exactly to the total. Ding merges against the baseline; we do not.

| | figure |
|---|--:|
| net-new (domain, year) pairs vs merged260730 | **96,522** |
| over unique domains | 81,885 |
| domains absent from the baseline in every year | **1,252** |
| English-verified pairs | **93** |
| judged and disqualified, with a per-item reason | 48 |
| not yet reached by the engine | 96,381 |
| candidate pool (evidence of existence, no year) | 41,102 |
| integrity invariants, all passing | 12 |

The English-verified figure is small and is a *rate*, not a ceiling. Section 6 explains what it will
be by Monday and what bounds it.

### Counting unit, stated before any comparison

merged260730 ships **10,263,632 raw lines**. Collapsed to registered domains under SPEC III.8 that
is **8,633,505 pairs over 5,490,102 domains**, a 1,630,127 difference (15.9%) from subdomains
folding into their registered domain. Our headline and a line-count of the same files will therefore
never agree, and the gap is a counting-unit difference rather than a discrepancy.

## 3. The English-website verification engine

### What the rule is, and what it is applied to

> "a domain must belong to an English-language website or to a website where English accounts for
> more than 50% of reliably classified body text across the sampled captures for the relevant year"

Applied at website level, from archived page body text, explicitly not from the spelling of the
domain or its TLD.

### How a verdict is produced

Per (domain, year): ask the CDX index for in-year captures anywhere under the domain, choose the
largest few by the stored length the index reports, fetch them as **raw bytes** in the Wayback
`id_` form so the archive's own page furniture is never in the sample, decode with
`charset_normalizer` rather than assuming UTF-8, strip markup to body text, and classify with
`py3langid`.

Four decisions inside that are worth stating because each one changes the answer:

- **Raw bytes, not UTF-8 text.** 1990s pages are frequently latin-1, Shift-JIS or GB2312 with no
  declared charset. Decoded as UTF-8 they become mojibake, mojibake classifies as undetermined, and
  undetermined pages leave the denominator, which would silently *raise* the measured English share.
- **Under 200 characters, or under 0.50 confidence, is "not reliably classified"** and leaves the
  denominator entirely rather than counting as non-English. Counting them would drag genuinely
  English sites out.
- **Captures are weighted by classified text length**, so a substantial English page outweighs a
  one-line non-English redirect notice instead of each counting once.
- **Strictly greater than 0.50 admits.** An exact half fails, per the wording.

### Language is not an evidence type

Verdicts go to a separate `domain_language` table rather than into the evidence taxonomy. Every
`evidence_type` answers "did this domain exist in this year"; a language verdict answers "what was
this website", which is orthogonal, and a domain can be perfectly evidenced and still inadmissible.

Each verdict stores **the exact snapshot URLs it read**. That is the whole difference between this
and a TLD prior: any verdict in `additions_english/<year>.csv` can be refetched and recomputed by
the reader.

### The engine was audited twice, and what that found

This section exists because a reviewer should be able to see how the method was tested, not only
what it produced. Two independent adversarial reviews were run against the engine on 1 August, one
briefed to find pairs that could reach the English files wrongly, the other to find pairs that could
be wrongly excluded. Every finding was reproduced before being accepted, and the most serious were
checked against live archived pages.

**Ten defects were confirmed and fixed.** The four worth naming here, because each one had already
put a wrong domain in a shipped file:

- **The archive can answer with a different year.** `web.archive.org` responds to a replay request
  it cannot serve exactly with a redirect to the nearest capture in time, in any year, and the HTTP
  client followed it silently and reported success. Verified live: a request for the 1997 capture of
  `1697.com` was answered with the capture of 17 October 2000. The engine recorded the URL it had
  *asked* for, so a reviewer refetching it would have received the same substitution and seen
  agreement. **The audit trail would have confirmed the error rather than exposed it**, which is the
  worst property a provenance record can have. The fetcher now reports which URL answered, a sample
  from the wrong year is discarded, and the stored URL is the one that was served.
- **A keyword link farm was admitted as an English website.** `2000s.com` for 2001 scored English at
  confidence 1.000 on 1,060 characters of comma-separated category names. The placeholder test
  returned early for any page over 1,000 characters, so it never looked.
- **A domain was certified English on a vendor login screen.** `1stflatrate.com` for 2001 was decided
  on an Ipswitch IMail page served from port 8383. Selecting the largest capture under a domain
  reliably finds third-party application chrome, whose English is the vendor's interface rather than
  the site's content.
- **Two domains were admitted on a `robots.txt` and nothing else.** The index labels it as HTML with
  status 200, and on a small site it is often longer than the homepage.

**All 297 verdicts held at that point were discarded rather than shipped**, and their journals kept
so every discarded verdict remains reproducible. Coverage returned to zero and rebuilt on the
corrected engine. The same decision was taken once before in this project, for the same reason: the
entire claim of this route is that a verdict is checkable, and a checkable verdict that is wrong is
worse than none.

Verified live after the fixes, on the four pairs above: the link farm and a "we have moved"
forwarding notice are now `non_site_text`, the `robots.txt` pair reads seven real pages instead, and
`1stflatrate.com` remains English but on the site's own pages. That last one is the outcome to
notice: a correct admission was kept, on correct evidence.

**Verdicts now carry the version of the engine that produced them**, only current-engine verdicts can
enter an annual file, and a pair leaves the work queue only when asking again could not change the
answer. Before that, any verdict at all removed a pair permanently, which is precisely what made
each defect above survive its own discovery.

### What the engine refuses to decide

This is the part most worth a reviewer's attention, because it is where a language classifier
usually overclaims.

A pair is left **unsettled**, with no verdict written at all, whenever the question did not actually
land: the index query failed, or captures existed and none of them could be fetched. Recording
either as "undetermined" would permanently exclude a possibly-English domain on the strength of a
transport error. This project has made that exact mistake once before, in the RDAP engine, at a cost
of 12,888 domains.

**And "the archive holds no capture for this domain in this year" is only ever recorded after
asking that question without filters.** The capture query filters on `statuscode:200` and
`mimetype:text/html`, so a year holding only redirects or plain text answers it empty. Treating that
emptiness as "nothing exists" would disqualify a domain on a question that was never asked, so an
empty filtered result now triggers a second, completely unfiltered index probe. Three outcomes, all
measured: nothing at all (`no_capture_in_year`), something but not readable HTML
(`no_readable_html_capture`), or the probe itself failed, in which case the pair stays unsettled.

### Adopted: Usenet announcement archives

Giganews donated its Usenet archive to the Internet Archive. Announcement and commerce groups carry
a posting date beside the URLs in each message, and **the date is intrinsic to the artifact rather
than recovered from a crawl**. That property, not Usenet as such, is what makes the source valuable:
it reaches years the archive's own coverage does not, which is exactly where this collection was
weakest.

**Admission rule, because a URL in a message body is typed by a human.** The corpus contains
`weddinqnetwork.com` and `dmjbuisness.co.uk`, and 35.4% of never-before-seen names are within one
edit of a name the store already holds. So a domain another source already places in an annual file
is real and only its year is open, and the post dates it (`dated_directory`); a name appearing only
in Usenet has neither its existence nor its year independently attested and goes to the candidate
pool (`link_target`) to earn its own evidence. The test is "appears in `domain_year`", not "appears
in `domain`", or a typo recorded as a candidate by an earlier round would corroborate itself.

**The rule is enforced, not just stated.** Measured against the store: of the Usenet-sourced pairs
in the annual files, **zero** rest on a domain that no other source attests. Every one is a year
added to a domain some independent route had already established, which is exactly what the
corroboration split is for. The names Usenet found on its own are in the candidate pool, where they
await a capture of their own.

**A parser finding worth carrying to anyone else using this corpus.** The donation rewrote a large
share of `Date:` headers as a bare `YYYY/MM/DD`, which `email.utils.parsedate_to_datetime` rejects
outright: **21,346 of 23,282 messages** in `comp.infosystems.www.announce`. Before that was handled
the route looked worth 913 pairs with nothing before 2000; after it, 6,885 across all six years from
the same files. A source can appear barren purely because of a header format.

And group size does not predict in-window content. `alt.www.webmaster` is 170 MB and entirely 2006
to 2013, so out-of-window and unreadable dates are counted separately, and group selection is by
name and expected yield rather than by size.

### Rejected: NYPW first-capture index. Estimated 27,276 net-new domains, measured 53

The estimate compared NYPW's **registered domains** against **raw hostname lines** from the
**phase-1** baseline. Both errors inflate. Measured against the current store: 2,413,003 in-window
pairs over 2,354,914 domains, of which all but **53** are already held, a 99.998% overlap, which is
what a sample of the Internet Archive's own CDX should look like against a baseline already drawn
from it. Two minutes of measurement avoided a 19.35 GB download. Reproducible via
`scripts/measure_nypw_yield.py`.

### Rejected: Australian Web Archive. 0 of 60 sampled domains had an in-window capture

Worth recording the endpoint correction even though the source failed:
`webarchive.nla.gov.au/awa/cdx` serves an anti-bot challenge, but **`web.archive.org.au/awa/cdx`
answers normally**, so the earlier rejection in `sources.md` was stale. The pool looked strong,
29,595 PANDORA domains in no annual file, and a clean 60-domain sample returned zero transport
failures and zero in-window captures. PANDORA's selective harvesting is simply later than our window
for the long tail.

### Method note, which is the point of this section

An initial estimate for one vein put it at 1,000 to 5,000 net-new domains; measuring it returned 5.
**Three of five sources assessed were rejected after measurement contradicted the estimate, two of
them by two orders of magnitude.** Estimates in this space are unreliable enough that every figure
in this report is measured against the store, and the measuring scripts ship in the archive so the
numbers can be re-derived rather than trusted.

## 5. The two shipped sets, and every exclusion

### The partition

`additions_english/` and `additions_unverified/` are disjoint and sum to `additions/`. Two integrity
checks assert that against the shipped files, and `verify.sh` re-checks it from inside the archive
with no dependencies beyond `python3`.

A pair sits outside the English set for one of two very different reasons:

- **`disqualified`**: the archive was asked and answered, and the pair failed the standard. Every
  one carries a reason and a row in `additions_unverified/disqualified.csv`.
- **`unchecked`**: the engine has not reached it. **No claim is made about its language, and none
  about whether the archive holds a capture for it.**

### The disqualification register

Every judged rejection is listed individually with the pages that were read, so any exclusion can be
inspected and disputed. The reason vocabulary is closed:

| Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `no_capture_in_year` | asked unfiltered, the archive holds nothing for that domain in that year |
| `no_readable_html_capture` | captures exist, none of them readable HTML we can classify |
| `insufficient_text` | under 200 characters of body text in every sample |
| `non_site_text` | a parking or holding page, not a website |
| `low_confidence` | text present, classification below 0.50 in every sample |
| `other_language` | classified, no English at all |
| `mixed_below_threshold` | English present and measured, but not a majority |

`other_language` and `mixed_below_threshold` are separated on purpose. Both fail, and they fail
differently: a reviewer weighing whether the 50% line sits in the right place needs to see how many
pairs are near it rather than nowhere near it.

## 6. Coverage, rate, and what the numbers will be on Monday

The English-verified count is small because verification is bound by how fast `web.archive.org` will
answer, not by anything in the pipeline. Stating that with measurements rather than adjectives:

**Measured throughput.** Three consecutive 400-pair batches at 2 workers with a 1.5 s floor ran at
6.56, 5.13 and 5.71 pairs per minute, a mean of **344 pairs/hour**, while a second engine was
competing for the same host. With that engine stopped and the whole budget given to verification, the
next batch measured **429 pairs/hour**. Between 57% and 62% of classified pairs come back English.

**Projection to Monday 12:00 UTC**, from a 48.8-hour window:

| Assumption | Classified | English expected |
|---|--:|--:|
| measured rate sustained | 16,800 | 9,600 to 10,400 |
| 70% duty, allowing for throttling and refusals | 11,750 | 6,700 to 7,300 |

The lower row is the one to plan against. The archive has refused this project three times, twice
overnight, and the supervisor backs off for up to 30 minutes when it does.

**The ceiling, stated plainly.** 24,148 of the 96,522 additions (25.0%) have a known in-year capture
in our own evidence, and those are the pairs most likely to earn a verdict. Even at the optimistic
rate the engine does not exhaust that subset by Monday. **English verification of this collection is
a multi-week job at the pace the archive permits**, and the honest reading of this round's English
figure is a rate with a projection attached, not a final count.

Two clarifications that matter for how the 25% is read:

- It is *not* a claim that the other 75% have no capture. It means only that we have not asked. A
  pair we have not asked about is reported as `unchecked`, never as a rejection.
- The share fell from 66.7% last round because the Usenet route added 60,559 pairs that are dated by
  a post rather than by a crawl. That is the source working as intended, and it dilutes a ratio whose
  denominator grew.

## 7. Completeness

The rule is that a completeness recommendation is permitted only when a submission adds fewer than
10,000 records in total **and** less than 0.1% after deduplication, both conditions, and the result
persists after genuinely new source families have been tested.

| Year | Additions | Growth vs baseline | Under 10,000? | Under 0.1%? |
|---|--:|--:|:-:|:-:|
| 1996 | 10,076 | 1.65% | no | no |
| 1997 | 15,571 | 1.23% | no | no |
| 1998 | 25,338 | 2.72% | no | no |
| 1999 | 14,071 | 0.93% | no | no |
| 2000 | 19,012 | 1.25% | no | no |
| 2001 | 12,454 | 0.44% | no | no |

**Neither threshold is met in any year, so no year is a candidate for completeness.** 1996 and 1997
were below 10,000 additions in the previous round and are no longer, because a genuinely new source
family reopened both. That is the behaviour section 5 anticipates: an apparent plateau was a limit of
the methods in use, not of the material.

## 8. The infrastructure this builds on, briefly

Covered in the previous report and reviewed, so only the parts a reader needs in order to interpret
the above.

**The evidence wall.** `domain_year.evidence_id` is `NOT NULL` and references a specific evidence
row, so an unevidenced year assignment cannot be expressed. Evidence types are split into
master-eligible and candidate-only, and the assignment helper refuses candidate-only evidence.
Anything with evidence of existence but not of a year goes to the candidate pool and stays out of
the annual files.

**Counting unit.** The registered domain under SPEC III.8, via a pinned Public Suffix List snapshot
so the result does not depend on the day it was run.

**Reproducibility, in three tiers.** Verify the shipped result from checksums and the evidence
manifest (~10 s, no network); rebuild the annual files from the shipped Parquet evidence graph
byte-identically (~1 min, no source data); or rebuild from the original sources (a download, then
~20 minutes). The archive's `README.md` gives the commands for each.

**The integrity gate.** Twelve invariants over the store, run by `ark check`, non-zero exit on any
failure. They encode rules this report states, so a reader who doubts one can run the gate rather
than take it on trust. All twelve pass on the shipped data.

## 9. Limitations

- **English verification covers a small share of the additions so far.** Section 6 gives the rate and
  the projection. The set will grow; the honest figure today is the one in the table.
- **The 1,252 domains absent from the baseline entirely are the smaller and stricter claim.** The
  96,522 headline is mostly years gained on domains the baseline already holds, which is what
  gap-filling produces by construction.
- **Usenet URLs are human-typed.** The corroboration split is the mitigation, and it is why 15,295
  Usenet-discovered names sit in the candidate pool rather than in the annual files.
- **A capture proves the site was archived that year, not that it was live all year.** The evidence
  supports presence, not continuous operation.
- **Verdicts are point-in-time readings of specific pages.** A site sampled at two captures may have
  been different at a third. The snapshot URLs ship so that any verdict can be re-examined.
- **48 pairs were judged before the reason vocabulary existed** and were requeued rather than shipped
  with an invented reason. They will re-appear with a reason once the engine reaches them again.

## 10. What is worth doing next

1. **Keep the verification engine running.** It is the binding constraint on the admissible set and
   the only thing that converts existing work into shippable additions. At the measured rate the
   capture-backed subset alone is roughly two more weeks.
2. **Verify the candidate pool into net-new domains.** 41,102 names, 15,295 of them Usenet-discovered
   and never seen by this project. A capture is what promotes one, and the measured hit rate on that
   population is 62%. This is the only route that moves the strict net-new-domain count, and it was
   stopped this round only because it competes with English verification for the same host.
3. **More sources with dates intrinsic to the artifact.** The Usenet finding generalises: what worked
   was not Usenet, it was that the date does not depend on a crawl. Anything with a dated record and
   a URL beside it deserves the same assessment, and 574 of the 628 shortlisted Usenet groups are
   still unprocessed.
